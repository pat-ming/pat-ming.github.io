About

I’m an undergraduate studying Computer Science and Mathematics at Washington University in St. Louis, expecting to graduate in December 2028.

My work sits at the point where reinforcement learning meets problems that are hard to specify: designing reward signals that can’t be gamed, building environments where correctness is actually checkable, and understanding why RL agents fail in ways that supervised models don’t. Most recently I spent a summer at Qognitive in New York working on hybrid RL algorithms and quantum algorithmic cooling under a Department of Energy–funded project.

Before that, and alongside it, I’ve been building things that are useful to people around me — a retrieval system that cuts WashU’s faculty-research search from three hours to five minutes, and a benchmark for mathematical construction tasks that current frontier models still find genuinely hard.

I’m always glad to talk about research, and I’m looking for opportunities in machine learning research and quantitative work. The fastest way to reach me is mingp@wustl.edu.

Research Interests

  • Post-training and deep reinforcement learning — reward design, verifier robustness, and algorithms that stay stable where SAC and PPO do not.
  • Evaluation that resists gaming — building benchmarks scored by hand-written verifiers rather than LLM judges, then red-teaming those verifiers until they hold.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation — multi-vector retrieval over structured domain corpora.
  • Applied machine learning — computer vision, and computational methods in finance.

Selected Work

RL Environment for Mathematical Construction Tasks

2026 – present

An 86-task reinforcement learning environment and benchmark spanning linear algebra, algebra, and analysis, scored by 28 hand-designed verifiers with no LLM in the loop. Red-teaming surfaced 735 wrong-object acceptances before the verifiers held; MiniMax-M2 reaches 79% on the hardest tier.

WUSTL Research Matchmaker

2026

A multi-vector RAG system that matches students to faculty by research fit, turning a three-hour manual search into a five-minute query. Built on a custom scraper covering 1,584 WashU STEM faculty profiles. Being prepared for use in first-year STEM courses.

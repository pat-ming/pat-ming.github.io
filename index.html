About

I’m an undergraduate studying Computer Science and Mathematics at Washington University in St. Louis. (Aug 2025 - Exp. Dec 2028)

I’m deeply interested in reinforcement learning, and how to leverage its strengths to build strong and reliable AI systems with post training. This summer, I interned at Qognitive in New York. There, I worked on building a new RL algorithms for quantum algorithmic cooling under a U.S. Department of Energy funded project. I engineered a hybrid algorithm that outperformed both vanilla SAC and PPO on 9 out of 11 gymnasium environments.

Other work I’ve done have been spent on building things that are useful to me and people around me. A RAG based WashU research faculty matchmaker that turns a PI search from three hours to five minutes, and most recently, a Claude plugin that optimizes my building workflow.

I’m always glad to talk about research, and I’m looking for opportunities in machine learning research and quantitative work. The fastest way to reach me is mingp@wustl.edu.

Research Interests

  • Post-training and deep reinforcement learning — reward design, verifier robustness, and algorithms that stay stable where SAC and PPO do not.
  • Evaluation that resists gaming — building benchmarks scored by hand-written verifiers rather than LLM judges, then red-teaming those verifiers until they hold.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation — multi-vector retrieval over structured domain corpora.
  • Applied machine learning — computer vision, and computational methods in finance.

Selected Work

Agentic Workflow Claude Plugin

2026 – present

A Claude Code plugin that autonomously builds entire projects end-to-end from a single prompt, encoding my personal development workflow. A hand-written orchestrator decomposes the request into a dependency-ordered plan, freezes a machine-checkable verifier for each phase, then delegates implementation to fresh headless agent sessions that coordinate entirely through git and the filesystem. Custom hooks enforce the verifiers as a tamper-proof contract — so the build agents can’t grade their own work — while bespoke subagents handle verifier authoring, cleanup, and finalization. Runs unattended for hours and pings my phone when it finishes or stalls.

RL Environment for Mathematical Construction Tasks

2026 – present

An 314-task reinforcement learning environment and benchmark spanning linear algebra, algebra, and analysis, scored by 28 hand-designed verifiers with no LLM in the loop. Red-teaming surfaced 735 wrong-object acceptances before the verifiers held; MiniMax-M2 reaches 79% on the hardest tier.
